00:00 Приветствие (Greeting)

Dmitry Elovsky opens the weekly programme "Чисто конкретная политика" and introduces political scientist Vladimir Pastukhov, as usual. Pastukhov asks what the coming day holds; they agree to start instead with what the past week brought.

00:29 Путин и его SMM-выступление (Putin's social-media-style appearance)

Elovsky reads Putin's remarks to the press: Russia had said talks could resume after the Duma elections, but on election day there was an attempted massive strike on Moscow and strikes on polling stations, one killing an election commission chair, so no talks. Pastukhov calls the impromptu Q&A format a product of the "clip thinking" era: long Soviet-style reports no longer work, so the Kremlin produces short, easily cut fragments. He sees it as a technical change showing only that the presidential administration seeks the shortest path from signal to mass circulation.

03:25 Кто кого нагнет. Или перетянет (Who forces whom, or outlasts whom)

Pastukhov says he heard both Putin and Zelensky in full and sees nothing new: each side wants to force the other onto its own terms. Zelensky, in his reading, wants a settlement before winter with Trump's and Europe's help and to appear to have forced Putin into talks; the Kremlin will try to look like the one forcing talks while delaying to carry out its winter plans. Putin's irritation is emotional but secondary. His routine-case scenario: no talks, a winter contest of front versus rear. Trump could intervene and seat them at the table if he really wants.

06:24 Действительно ли Трамп предложил Зеленскому Москву (Did Trump really suggest Moscow to Zelensky?)

On a newspaper report that Trump suggested Zelensky go to Moscow, Pastukhov doubts it is an accurate account rather than propaganda or counter-propaganda; he says he no longer trusts what he hears or even sees. From Zelensky's press conference he concludes the conversation did not go well: Ukraine gets only remaining, already-allocated funds; silence on missile defence, additional Patriots and action against Putin; Trump keeps returning to a territorial compromise. If Trump said it, it was probably in an ambiguous form, since saying it directly would be a scandal.

09:17 Важное объявление и привет из пятницы (Announcement and channel business)

Elovsky offers a mock "free interpretation" of how Trump might have phrased it, asks viewers to like and subscribe because of YouTube's algorithm, and jokes with Pastukhov that, having been labelled foreign agents again, there is no room left on screen for all the labels.

10:14 Трамп и его интерес с Путиным (Trump's interest in Putin)

Asked about the invitation for Putin to the G20 in Miami in December, Pastukhov calls it, with high probability, trolling of Europeans and a test of reactions, which Europe swallowed cautiously. The real substance: talk of a new Putin–Trump meeting, with Iran and China among topics. Zelensky, he says, signals that a bilateral meeting with Putin is impossible and suggested using Trump as cover; Trump avoids proposing a three-way meeting directly because the Kremlin's answer is terms first, then a meeting.

12:40 Новая война в Европе — в следующем году (A new war in Europe next year)

Pastukhov states his view that Europe is moving steadily toward a big war around summer–autumn of next year and nothing indicates otherwise. If all players accept it as fate, nothing changes; if a red light starts flashing, a chance of simultaneous emergency braking remains, with Trump's role decisive. Trump's will alone is insufficient: while Europe, Ukraine and Russia each want peace only on their own terms, there is no room for him; if they become ready for compromise terms, he could serve as mediator.

14:35 На кого нападет Путин (Whom will Putin attack?)

Elovsky lists three news items: El Mundo, citing the CIA, says Russia may attack Mediterranean Europe; RIA Novosti published and within an hour deleted an article about a short special operation in Lithuania; Putin said Russian speakers are oppressed in the Baltics but Russia is using diplomatic means. Pastukhov says only Putin's answer matters. He calls the El Mundo report a ChatGPT-style fantasy: Russia could attack from anywhere, as Ukraine's Operation Spiderweb showed, and Italian and French officials said they never received such CIA information.

18:10 РИА «Новости» сделали больше, чем их просили (RIA Novosti overdid it)

Pastukhov describes the RIA article's author as one of a long-standing ultranationalist fringe (he names the Malofeev, Dugin, Zatulin circle), comparable to the 10–15% such people in any country. State media, influenced by people from that milieu and tasked with trolling the Baltics, went further than asked and then pulled it; Lavrov and Medvedev had already said the same. He says Putin's press appearances have almost no random questions or answers, so raising the rights of Russian speakers was deliberate, to link the Baltics to Ukraine. The Kremlin lacks the means for a Baltic operation for now; a latent mobilisation of 300.000 could change that, but not alongside Donbas.

23:06 Какой будет война в Европе (What the war in Europe will look like)

Pastukhov says such regimes can take a very long time to die. He sees no resources for a direct Baltic operation unless pressure on Ukraine is strategically reduced, which would be visible in advance. Near term he expects hybrid war, already under way: deniable sabotage, psychological warfare, efforts to stir protest and consolidate pro-Russian forces in Europe. Europe plays "come take it", Putin plays "prove it". After a lag, Europe will conclude that the only adequate answer to sabotage is counter-sabotage, bringing the world to a line where any wrong move leads to direct multilateral war.

27:21 Итоги выборов. Победа Володина (Election results: Volodin stays)

Pastukhov explains he watched Volodin as a marker between two post-election scenarios: permafrost (minor reshuffles, no elite turnover) and warming (a generational shift preparing for something bigger, ending the war or escalation). Warming would have started by moving people such as Matvienko and Shoigu to sinecures, letting Volodin or Dyumin move up. Volodin staying signals Putin intends nothing extraordinary for now.

30:24 Вопросы зрителей. Вторая мировая война (Viewer question: WWII)

A viewer, Timur, asks about post-Soviet debates on reframing WWII and Victory Day. Pastukhov says he cannot be objective: had the war ended differently, he would not exist. Humanity fought Nazism, a malignant tumour whose victory would likely have led to planetary catastrophe. Good and evil can both be ambiguous, but there are limits to relativism and to revisionism; historical good defeated historical evil.

33:34 Мода на «Прекрасную Россию будущего» спала (The fashion for a Wonderful Russia of the Future has passed)

To a viewer asking whether émigré talk of a democratic, federal future Russia is folklore, Pastukhov says the fashion has strongly faded and the question is about five years late, but discussing it is still worth it: a bifurcation point will come, and then whoever can justify an idea and make people believe it matters. He cites the voucher idea, born in theory a decade before privatisation and grabbed because it was the top card on the table. Elovsky notes the Bolsheviks also formed in emigration; Pastukhov adds that images of the future are born in periods of reaction, which exist to give time to think.
