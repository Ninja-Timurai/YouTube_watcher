# AI models can now help run physical science experiments

_Channel: Anthropic · Published: 2026-08-27 · Length: 11:11 · Transcript: captions (en) · Summarised: 2026-10-01_  
_Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P1zBiAQU1IA_

## Bottom line

Anthropic presents Model Hardware Standard (MHS), a way for Claude to operate lab devices, built with neuroscientist Arco Bast. Demos cover microscopes, a robot arm and a Genentech liquid-handling experiment. The claim: experiment setup, said to take maybe 80% of a scientist's time, could shrink from years to months.

## Key takeaways

- **The bottleneck:** building experiments "takes maybe 80% of a scientist's time" — device setup and debugging, not science. [00:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P1zBiAQU1IA&t=21s)
- **The idea:** each device speaks a different language; MHS is a general way for AI to talk to any of them. [01:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P1zBiAQU1IA&t=105s)
- **Safety limits:** a robot arm refuses Claude's move outside its defined safe range. [03:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P1zBiAQU1IA&t=207s)
- **Fallible, supervised:** a microscope scientist warns Claude "will make mistakes"; one broken sample costs weeks of work and thousands of dollars. [04:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P1zBiAQU1IA&t=290s)
- **Faster science:** a speaker expects a PhD student's two-year setup to take two months; Genentech tests closed-loop drug-discovery experiments. [07:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P1zBiAQU1IA&t=477s)
