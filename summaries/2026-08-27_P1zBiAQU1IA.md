# AI models can now help run physical science experiments

_Channel: Anthropic · Published: 2026-08-27 · Length: 11:11 · Transcript: captions (en) · Summarised: 2026-10-01_  
_Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P1zBiAQU1IA_

## Bottom line

Anthropic presents Model Hardware Standard (MHS), a general way for Claude to talk to and operate lab devices, built with neuroscientist Arco Bast. Demos show Claude driving a microscope, a robot arm, a Leica microscope and a liquid-handling experiment at Genentech. The claim: experiment setup, said to take about 80% of a scientist's time, could shrink from years to months.

## Key takeaways

- **The problem:** the narrator says building experiments "takes maybe 80% of a scientist's time", work that is not science itself — devices, setup, hardware and software debugging. [00:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P1zBiAQU1IA&t=21s)
- **The idea:** each lab device "speaks" a different language; Arco Bast found a way to connect any two devices, which became Model Hardware Standard, a general way for AI to interact with devices. [01:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P1zBiAQU1IA&t=105s)
- **Built-in safety limits:** a safe range is defined for a robot arm, and a movement outside it requested of Claude is shown being refused by MHS. [03:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P1zBiAQU1IA&t=207s)
- **Claude is fallible and must be supervised:** a Danaher/Leica scientist stresses Claude "will make mistakes" and that breaking a sample worth weeks of work and thousands of dollars ends the experiment. [04:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P1zBiAQU1IA&t=284s)
- **Fast prototyping:** Claude wrote a tracking program with a UI that followed swimming algae for minutes; a participant says a PhD student could get such a setup running in two months instead of two years. [07:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P1zBiAQU1IA&t=477s)
- **Closed-loop drug discovery:** at Genentech, Claude runs operations, reads results and adjusts parameters to reduce bubbles in liquid transfer; speakers call it the first time AI interacts with the physical world in drug discovery. [09:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P1zBiAQU1IA&t=571s)
- **Open horizon:** a speaker says it is "very difficult for us to predict" how AI and MHS will affect the world 30, 50 years out, while others name drug development, quantum computing and nuclear fusion as targets. [10:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P1zBiAQU1IA&t=626s)

## Section by section

### [00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P1zBiAQU1IA&t=0s) From theory to hardware, and the MHS origin
The narrator frames the bottleneck: theories need physical experiments, and building them consumes most of a scientist's time. Joining Anthropic with a "pie in the sky" vision of AI running experiments, the narrator met neuroscientist Arco Bast, who studies memory formation in the brain and was setting up a difficult imaging experiment with a custom-built microscope. Bast's insight was a way to make devices with different "languages" talk to each other. Seeing it work, the narrator realised it could let AI run any experiment, and the two built Model Hardware Standard. Tests follow: a robot arm with a defined safe range refuses an out-of-range move, grabs an object after being asked from scratch, and the work moves to a Leica microscope with Danaher, where the scientist warns Claude will make mistakes and samples are costly.

### [05:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P1zBiAQU1IA&t=335s) Microscopy, algae tracking and Genentech
On the Leica microscope, Claude is described as aware that switching to higher magnification can crash the lens into the sample; it produces a focused, false-coloured image and identifies lignified cell walls. The team proposes treating Claude as a colleague. Claude then writes a script, and at a participant's request a UI, to track swimming algae that a scientist would otherwise follow for hours; it tracks for several minutes. A speaker says this could cut setup from two years to two months, freeing time for biological questions. At Genentech, Claude runs a closed-loop liquid-handling experiment to reduce bubbles, which speakers frame as "more shots on the goal" in drug discovery. The video closes on hopes for drug development, quantum computing and nuclear fusion.
