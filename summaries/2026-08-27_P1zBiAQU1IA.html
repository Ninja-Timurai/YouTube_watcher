Bottom line

Anthropic presents Model Hardware Standard (MHS), a general way for Claude to talk to and operate lab devices, built with neuroscientist Arco Bast. Demos show Claude driving a microscope, a robot arm, a Leica microscope and a liquid-handling experiment at Genentech. Section by section

00:00 From theory to hardware, and the MHS origin

The narrator frames the bottleneck: theories need physical experiments, and building them consumes most of a scientist's time. Joining Anthropic with a "pie in the sky" vision of AI running experiments, the narrator met neuroscientist Arco Bast, who studies memory formation in the brain and was setting up a difficult imaging experiment with a custom-built microscope. Bast's insight was a way to make devices with different "languages" talk to each other. Seeing it work, the narrator realised it could let AI run any experiment, and the two built Model Hardware Standard. Tests follow: a robot arm with a defined safe range refuses an out-of-range move, grabs an object after being asked from scratch, and the work moves to a Leica microscope with Danaher, where the scientist warns Claude will make mistakes and samples are costly.

05:35 Microscopy, algae tracking and Genentech

On the Leica microscope, Claude is described as aware that switching to higher magnification can crash the lens into the sample; it produces a focused, false-coloured image and identifies lignified cell walls. The team proposes treating Claude as a colleague. Claude then writes a script, and at a participant's request a UI, to track swimming algae that a scientist would otherwise follow for hours; it tracks for several minutes. A speaker says this could cut setup from two years to two months, freeing time for biological questions. At Genentech, Claude runs a closed-loop liquid-handling experiment to reduce bubbles, which speakers frame as "more shots on the goal" in drug discovery. The video closes on hopes for drug development, quantum computing and nuclear fusion.
